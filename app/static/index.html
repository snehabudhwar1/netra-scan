How it works

Three steps happen in a few seconds. The photo only moves on to the next step if the one before it went well.

  1. Check the photo

    Blur, darkness, glare, a cut-off retina and "this is not a retina" are measured first. A photo that fails is not graded. You see the reason and how to retake it.

  2. Read the photo

    About 45 measurements of blood vessels, bright spots, dark spots and colour are combined with a numeric description from a pre-trained image network. A statistical machine-learning model turns those numbers into a grade.

  3. Say how sure it is

    You get a grade from 0 to 4, the chance that an eye-doctor visit is needed, and a clear "uncertain" label when the photo is unlike the photos the model learned from or the model is not confident.

Limits and privacy

  • It is not a diagnosis. Only an eye doctor can diagnose diabetic retinopathy, and Netra Scan has not been tested on patients.
  • It learned from one public set of photos. On photos from a different clinic it usually does worse. The numbers above show by how much.
  • It is built for colour retina photos. Pictures of a screen, other parts of the eye and scans have not been tested.
  • Mild disease (grade 1) and the most advanced grades (3 and 4) are the hardest to name exactly.
  • The Contrast view and Vessel map are image filters to help a person look. They do not show what the model used to decide.

Your photo

The photo is processed in the server's memory to produce the result. It is not saved, and the result is not stored.

If a result worries you, or you have symptoms such as blurred vision, floaters or dark patches, see an eye doctor. Diabetes also needs regular eye check-ups even when a photo looks fine.
